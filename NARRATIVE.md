# NARRATIVE.md — the narrative architecture of the Ouroboros Manifold site

This document fixes **what the site says and in what order**. It is the structure-and-content brief; `DESIGN.md` is the look-and-feel brief. Read both. The division of labor is deliberate:

- **This file decides:** the page structure, the narrative progression, which aspects of the game get a showcase and in what sequence, and every factual claim about the game.
- **The design tool decides:** everything visual — theme, palette, layout, typography, motion, and above all the **concept images and animations** for each section. That is the job: *visual brush and concept art against a fixed narrative and fixed facts.*

Do not restructure the arc, reorder the showcases, or introduce game facts that are not in the **Facts** section below. Where earlier mockups did that, they were wrong (see **Do not say**).

## What the site is

The public face of *Ouroboros Manifold* — a multiplayer, generational, strategic real-time-strategy game set in a living, stochastic galaxy. Today it recruits and orients playtesters; those testers are early customers, and the site is designed to grow into the marketing site. Its job is to make a strategy-game player *feel* what this game is and want in — showing the general idea and one cool, concrete example per concept, never a firehose of information.

## Architecture

**A narrative home page, plus deeper pages.** The home page is the spine: a top-to-bottom story a visitor scrolls through. Three existing pages carry the detail and are linked from the relevant beat of the story:

| Route | What it is | Linked from |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | The narrative home (this arc) | — |
| `/layers/` | The live, interactive three.js demo of the layer-and-vision system | Beat 3 |
| `/offers/` | The twelve ranked bets about what makes the game worth playing, each with its own page | Beat 5 |

Keep these routes. The site is an Astro static build; the offers and roadmap copy is generated from the game's design documents, so those pages are styled around fixed content rather than rewritten.

## The narrative arc — the home page, top to bottom

The progression runs from the intimate to the vast: one commander and one mission, outward to a living galaxy, generations of history, and finally many eras played with friends. The biggest idea is **teased in the hero and paid off as the crescendo**. Each beat names the *concept moment* — the single image or animation that section wants. Those lines are the seeds for the concept-art pass; they say what to show, not how to draw it.

### 1. Hero — the fantasy in one breath

*You are a commander, not a king, in a galaxy that moves on its own.*

One line of the core fantasy, and a quiet tease of the crescendo: this is a game you play across eras, in one campaign, with your friends. A single call to action (join the playtest) and a pull toward the story below. No feature bullets, no metric strip of invented numbers.

**Concept moment:** a commander's task force dwarfed by galactic scale — a handful of ships against something enormous and indifferent.

### 2. Commander, not king

You are one person handed a task force and an impossible job by a civilization with its own plans, its own politics, and its own momentum. A force, a mission, a deadline. There is no base to build and no economy to manage; what you hold, you hold with what you brought and what you capture on the ground. The world does not revolve around you.

**Concept moment:** the task force in the field, its objective visible in the distance, the deadline told by the failing light — a force, an objective, a deadline, and no interface anywhere.

### 3. The mission is won on the ground you read

The tactical showcase, and the deepest section of the page. Two halves.

**The landscape engine and the sense of scale.** Maps are generated by an intelligent landscaping system that produces strategically deep terrain at a cinematic scale: chokepoints, high ground, approaches that reward reading the ground. Terrain, not a stat sheet, sets the skill ceiling. Combat is full real-time with no pause, one engagement per mission, thirty to sixty minutes of continuous play.

**Concept moment:** a vast, readable generated map at the game's low-poly scale — a chokepoint with high ground above a low approach, terrain legible at a glance.

**Layers and vision.** Every map is a stack of three to five vertical layers. Vision falls off with layer distance: a unit sees its own layer fully, an adjacent layer at half its sensor range, a layer two away at a fifth, and nothing three or more layers away. Surrounds and high ground decide fights; a smaller force that reads the layers beats a larger one that does not. This section links to the **live layer demo** at `/layers/`, which runs those real vision numbers.

**Concept moment:** none — the live demo at `/layers/` shows this better than a still could; this half of the beat frames the demo.

### 4. For the diehards — how the units balance

A deliberately smaller, deeper aside for the RTS crowd. Six unit types, two per role, in a soft rock-paper-scissors: Strike flanks Siege, Siege crushes Line, Line absorbs Strike — a triangle with exceptions, never a hard counter table. Each unit has exactly one ability. Every silhouette is readable on its own. A fight with the same seed plays out the same way, tick for tick, so what you learn from a loss is real.

**Concept moment:** the balance as positional geometry — wedges, blocks, and arrows, no units: the three relationships as a legend, one side reading the ground against one that doesn't, and the showpiece animation of both sides trading moves like a chess game.

### 5. The galaxy is alive

Underneath the strategy runs a genuinely stochastic simulation. Factions rise and fall, borders move, alliances form and break — whether or not you act, and never scripted. Between your missions the galaxy keeps turning, and it can surprise you. This section links to **the offers** at `/offers/`, where each of the game's bets is laid out with the hypothesis behind it.

**Concept moment:** a galaxy-scale strategic map — dozens of small systems joined by glowing route lines, some bright and thriving, others dim and contested — a living network, not a static board.

### 6. You return as someone new

Each mission is a chapter. Between chapters, twenty to a hundred years pass and the galaxy evolves through the simulation. You come back as someone new — a descendant, an old enemy's heir, a stranger raised by circumstance — into a world that remembers what happened even if your new character does not. The consequences of what you did persist. A chronicle records it all as a real history, different every campaign: written in the game's own register, elegiac and dry, never a notification log.

**Concept moment:** the same region shown twice, decades apart — fresh construction and a strong garrison, then battle scars, rebuilt structures, and a different faction's colors — beside a page of the in-universe chronicle.

### 7. The crescendo — many eras, one campaign, with your friends

The headline. The game ships across multiple eras built on one shared skeleton: maps, civilizations, and era systems are transferable, not bespoke content. A campaign can move chronologically through eras. And it is multiplayer: players who own the same eras meet on one shared map, in deterministic lockstep across Windows, macOS, and Linux, and a match can cross eras. The Settled Reach — the sci-fi, future era — is where the launch sits; other eras carry the same structure into other times.

**Concept moment:** two friends' task forces from two different eras meeting on one shared map, each era read entirely from the dress of its ships — same terrain, same strategic shape, two ages sharing it. No interface.

### 8. Join the playtest

The close. An honest invitation to join a small group of testers and tell the truth about something unfinished. Three rounds, each opened by evidence rather than a date: a handful of friends first, then a wider Discord cohort, then a Steam Playtest. Links to **the roadmap** at `/roadmap/`. The standing framing carries through: everything shown is a target the game is being built toward, and the testers are helping decide what gets built next.

**Concept moment:** none required — this is the call to action.

## Facts — quote these, invent nothing

Every claim on the site comes from the game's design documents. Use these numbers and names exactly; where a fact is not listed here, do not supply one.

- **Time between chapters:** twenty to one hundred years. Not thirty. Not a fixed figure.
- **Mission length:** thirty to sixty minutes of continuous real-time play, one engagement per mission, **no pause**. There are no "turns."
- **Six unit types**, two per role: Raider and Interceptor (Strike); Vanguard and Warden (Line); Bombard and Bastion (Siege). One ability each.
- **Cross-layer vision factors:** same layer full, adjacent layer 0.50, two layers away 0.20, three or more away not visible. Maps have **three to five** layers. The first playtest round ships a two-layer prototype.
- **Determinism** means reproducible given a seed — the same seed gives the same fight, tick for tick, byte-identical across **Linux, Windows, and macOS**. It does **not** mean the game has no randomness. The game's founding principle is *genuine stochasticity*: outcomes are truly uncertain, and the pitch is the tragedy of understanding — you can read the situation, make the right call, and it still might not be enough. Never present "deterministic combat" as a feature.
- **No base-building, no economy** is the correct framing. The only in-mission growth is reinforcement structures a player captures and holds; do not imply there is zero production.
- **The setting:** the Settled Reach is the sci-fi, *future* era — one launch era among past, present, and future on the shared skeleton, not the sole setting.
- **Platforms:** Linux, Windows, macOS. The first round is a Windows build shared over Discord; the third round is a Steam Playtest.
- **Tone:** elegiac, not grim — a real history being recorded, with tragedy, humor, beauty, and unexpected hope. The humor is dry, earned, occasionally absurd; it lives in the writing. The game winks; it does not mug.
- **The look of the game:** low-poly, blocky, toy-like, flat and warm, in the Art of Rally register — clean readable silhouettes, one per unit type, a cinematic sense of scale. The *site* can be as polished as the design wants; this describes the game art, not the web page.

## Do not say

These appeared in earlier mockups and are wrong or invented. Remove them wherever they occur and do not reintroduce them.

- "30 Earth Years," "Temporal Leap +30Y," or any fixed jump length — the real range is twenty to one hundred years.
- "100% Deterministic," "Zero RNG," "Not Dice Rolls," "deterministic ballistics" — contradicts the game's core principle of genuine uncertainty.
- "Zero Harvesters" as a headline metric, "fuel reserve management," "supply umbilical lines" — invented systems.
- "macOS & Windows Silicon Native," "Steam Keys Dispatched Weekly," "Direct Officer Discord Access" — invented platform and delivery claims.
- "State Directorate," "galactic state council," "officer@domain.mil," military-bureaucracy flavor text — not the game's voice.
- "© 2025 Ouroboros Systems," "An Apple-grade Generational Architecture," "Press Kit," "Manifesto" — invented branding and pages.
- Any specific ship date, release window, or version number ("Playtest v0.9," "Stage 1 Alpha"). Rounds open on evidence, not dates.
- Invented sensor jargon or a fake radar console. The layer system is shown by the real live demo at `/layers/` and by concept art of the real idea (stacked layers, vision falling off with distance).
- Any existing-IP name, logo, insignia, ship, or character. Thematic inspiration only. This is the one hard rule and it applies to every image.

## What is left to the design tool

All of it, visually. Theme and palette direction (start from `DESIGN.md`; that brief is open and invites bold options, so do not treat this file as re-litigating it), layout, typography, spacing, motion — and the main deliverable, **the concept image or animation for each beat's concept moment above**. The arc, the sequence, and the facts are fixed; how they look is the design's to invent.

A note on the images: each *concept moment* is a seed. The full per-moment prompts — medium, references, composition, negative lists — are authored in the companion prompts file (`CONCEPT_PROMPTS.md` in the site repo; `docs/northstar/mvp/site/concept_prompts.md` in the game repo), one per moment, with the fixed choices (static baseline plus five animations, the game's low-poly register, world-only, no text in images).
